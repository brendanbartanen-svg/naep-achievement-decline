## Terminology convention proposed for the whole revision (all agents)

- **Exposure**: "smartphones and digital media" (or "the exposure," "the candidate," "the hypothesis"). Never "the digital-media mechanism" or "the digital-media channel."
- **Pathway**: the behavioral route from exposure to achievement (time displacement; attention fragmentation; social displacement; degraded self-regulation). The word "pathway" currently appears **nowhere** in the paper; the taxonomy paragraph (EDIT A4-3) defines it on first use. Any agent introducing "pathway" earlier (abstract, intro) should stay consistent with the four-way split.
- **Channel**: reserved for design/aggregation margins and non-H2 routes (the "national, adoption-driven channel," the schooling-mode and attendance channels of the pandemic/absence story). Existing conforming uses at lines 237, 239, 372, 383 are left alone.
- **Mechanism**: removed from running prose. Its one surviving use is the taxonomy's opening sentence ("names an exposure, not a mechanism"), which rebuts critique 1b in its own vocabulary.

### EDIT A4-3 [insert-after]

SECTION: H2 (new capstone paragraph, inserted after the fertility-corroboration paragraph, before the Assessment line)
PROPOSED: \textbf{Exposure versus pathway.} ``Smartphones and digital media'' names an exposure, not a mechanism. If the exposure lowered achievement, it did so through one or more behavioral pathways, and the candidates differ: time displacement (screens crowd out reading, homework, and sleep); attention fragmentation (devices interrupt work in class and out of it); social displacement (less in-person interaction, the channel the fertility literature identifies); and degraded self-regulation (heavy use erodes the capacity to sustain effort). The evidence above speaks to each unevenly. The collapse of reading for fun (Figure~\ref{fig:fun}) is direct evidence of time displacement. The PISA distraction and notification items measure attention fragmentation. The time-diary shift from in-person socializing to digital leisure marks social displacement. And the ban studies' concentration of gains among previously low-achieving students reads most naturally as relief of distraction and weak self-regulation. This report establishes, at most, a reduced-form candidate: it does not adjudicate among these pathways. Its policy conclusions do not require that adjudication: the ban evidence and the attendance decompositions support binding school-day restrictions and attendance recovery whichever pathway dominates. The scientific account does require it, and that is where the empirical frontier sits.
WHY: D6 core deliverable: technology vs. pathway; the four candidate pathways; evidence-to-pathway map (reading-for-fun collapse -> displacement; PISA distraction/notification items -> fragmentation; time-diary -> social displacement; ban-study heterogeneity -> fragmentation/self-regulation); plain non-adjudication statement; policy conclusions do not require adjudication but the scientific account does. ~190 words; no numbers, no new citations; run-in bold matches H2's existing paragraph style.
RISK: none on content; anchor sits in the paragraph A3 (D7) may rewrite — reconciler should re-anchor to the final corroboration paragraph if so
