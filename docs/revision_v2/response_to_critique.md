# Response to External Review

**Report:** *Why Did American Student Achievement Decline? Verifying the Trends and Testing Competing Explanations with NAEP Data*
**Revision:** Version 2.0 (July 2, 2026), revising Version 1.9 (June 12, 2026)
**Repository:** github.com/brendanbartanen-svg/naep-achievement-decline (code, data, evidence files, full version history)

This memo responds point by point to the written review of v1.9. The review's points are paraphrased in the headings (the reviewer's text is not reproduced in this public repository); quoted phrases are the report's own v1.9 language, and indented block quotations are from the revised report. Locations are given by section name.

---

## What changed, and what did not

Version 2.0 revises what the paper claims and how it reads. The substantive changes:

- **Claim-strength calibration throughout.** Claim language is now tied to the evidence behind each sentence: verified data description, external causal literature, this report's own quasi-experimental nulls, associations, and synthesis ranking each get distinct verb families. The strongest synthesis language the paper now permits itself is "the leading candidate" / "the best-supported remaining candidate," always paired with the pending direct test. "Verdict" (14 occurrences in v1.9), "best explained by," and "uniquely predicts" appear zero times in v2.0.
- **The inferential strategy stated up front,** in the Introduction, in the report's own voice, rather than surfacing in Limitations.
- **Symmetric treatment of the two null designs** (NCLB-waiver timing and 4G-rollout timing): matched titles, matched five-part structure, matched sentence frames (Sharper Tests).
- **A new rival-hypothesis analysis:** deteriorating adolescent mental health, added as a named candidate with its own evidence block, scorecard row, synthesis treatment, and limitations entry (Testing the Hypotheses, H8; Synthesis; Limitations).
- **The conclusion aligned with the inseparability concession.** Because these data cannot separate the two candidates that clear the dual-criterion screen, the Conclusion frames phase one as a post-2012 transformation of adolescent life in which digital media is the better-evidenced driver and deteriorating adolescent mental health an entangled companion, rather than crowning a single candidate.
- **A pathway taxonomy** for the digital-media hypothesis, with terminology discipline separating exposure, pathway, and channel (H2; the section formerly titled "Mechanism Checks" is now "Channel Checks").
- **A full organizational and line-level edit:** abstract rebuilt at 300 words (from roughly 430), assertive headings recalibrated, the Limitations monolith restructured, Synthesis/Conclusion redundancy removed, a roadmap added to the Introduction.

What did not change: **no analyses, estimates, or data.** Every number in v1.9 is carried unchanged. The only additions are the sourced prevalence values behind the new mental-health analysis, three components of the existing Minnesota Common Core result surfaced as part of a correction described below (the components were already in the archived results file), and version stamps. One directional *reading* of an existing statistic was corrected and the correction is disclosed in the text (see "Changes beyond the review"). The report's disclosure paragraph ("How this report was produced") records the scope of the revision explicitly: a response to a written external review "with claim-strength calibration, symmetric treatment of the report's two null designs (waiver timing and 4G rollout), an added rival-hypothesis analysis (adolescent mental health), and a full organizational and line-level edit ... no analyses, estimates, or data changed."

## Point-by-point

### 1a. Consistency conflated with causation: the review holds that the paper shows compatibility with the patterns, not that smartphones outperform every alternative

Two things were true of v1.9. It stated its inferential status precisely — the Limitations section opened, verbatim, "This analysis is observational. The hypothesis tests rest on timing, distributional incidence, and cross-sectional comparisons, not experimental variation." And its abstract, assessment lines, synthesis, and conclusion carried more confidence than that status licenses. We do not concede that the report misunderstood the inference it was making; we do concede that the calibration lived in the wrong place and at the wrong volume, and that a reader should not have to reach Limitations to learn the rules the front of the paper is playing by. The fix is structural:

- The Introduction now owns the epistemic framework before any evidence is presented, including exactly the reframing the review asked for:

  > The output is a ranking of candidate explanations by their consistency with the evidence, not a causal estimate. Consistency is a screen, not proof, and the candidate list stays open: an explanation can lead the ranking and still be wrong.

  The same paragraph states that the report's strongest claims are its negative ones — about which explanations the evidence rejects — and that the positive conclusion is a ranking held provisionally, subject to direct tests as they arrive (Introduction).
- The abstract now ends: "The result is a consistency ranking, not a causal estimate."
- Every hypothesis assessment is re-tiered. H2's now reads "the leading candidate for phase one," names its four lines of support, carries this project's own null against it in the same breath, and closes: "This is a consistency ranking, not a causal estimate; the pre-registered state phone-ban test against NAEP 2026 is the next direct test" (Testing the Hypotheses, H2).
- The scorecard itself is captioned as a consistency record, and its notes state that consistency is a screen, not a causal attribution (Synthesis).

On the review's list of other social changes that accelerated during the 2010s, each named candidate is now addressed explicitly, in a place a reader will find (the Candidate Explanations section closes with a pointer to them; H8 opens with the dispatches):

- **Rising mental health problems.** Conceded as a genuine rival that v1.9 had treated only as an outcome of the device shock, not as a candidate cause. It is now a named candidate with a full evidence block: right timing, international reach, adolescent concentration; it passes the same screens that eliminate the school-policy hypotheses and, in its adolescent form, clears both bars of the paper's dual-criterion test. Its assessment: "the strongest unresolved rival to the digital-media hypothesis, and not separable from it with this report's data." Its scorecard row is deliberately identical to H2's, and the table's note says the two rows "are separated only by evidence outside the table" (H8; Synthesis; Limitations).
- **Increasing immigration.** This was answered in v1.9 by evidence rather than assertion — scores fell within every demographic group; reweighting bounds the compositional contribution at 1–2 points (H5); and the decline appears in Catholic schools, across rich countries, and among adults, populations and sectors immigration composition cannot reach. What was missing was a single place where a reader proposing immigration would find that answer assembled. The opening of H8 now provides it.
- **Shifts in school discipline.** v1.9 explicitly classified this as untested rather than rejected ("Avenues examined and set aside"). v2.0 moves that subsection to the end of Testing the Hypotheses, signposts it from the candidate list, and repeats the classification in the H8 dispatch: right timing, a bottom-concentrated prediction, and no clean policy variation independent of the variables already examined — untested, not rejected.
- **Political polarization.** Dispatched on stated grounds in H8: it names a real change but supplies no articulated pathway to a decline that is bottom-concentrated, adolescent-concentrated, internationally synchronized, and present among adults, and its intensity is distinctly American while the decline is not.

Finally, the logical point behind "not ... every potential alternative" — that elimination is only as strong as the enumerated candidates — is now a standing, stated limit rather than an implicit one. It appears at the candidate list ("the elimination logic used below is only as strong as the candidate set itself"), again in "What the tests jointly imply" ("a rival not on the list is untouched by the failures of those on it"), and in the Synthesis ranking itself, which describes digital media as "the best-supported remaining candidate for phase one, not an established cause."

### 1b. Smartphones are a technology, not a mechanism: if they affect achievement, it is through some pathway the paper leaves unspecified

Conceded, both terminologically and substantively. The paper contained pathway evidence throughout — the collapse of reading for fun, the PISA distraction and notification items, time-diary evidence on in-person socializing, the ban studies' effect heterogeneity — but never assembled it into a taxonomy or said plainly that it does not adjudicate among pathways. H2 now contains a dedicated passage, "Exposure versus pathway," that opens:

> "Smartphones and digital media" names an exposure, not a mechanism.

It names four candidate pathways — time displacement (screens crowd out reading, homework, and sleep), attention fragmentation, social displacement (the in-person-interaction channel the fertility literature identifies), and degraded self-regulation — maps the paper's existing evidence onto each, and states the boundary: "This report establishes, at most, a reduced-form candidate: it does not adjudicate among these pathways." It also separates what depends on that adjudication (the scientific account, which the passage identifies as the empirical frontier) from what does not (the policy conclusions on binding school-day restrictions and attendance recovery, which hold whichever pathway dominates).

The vocabulary is now disciplined paper-wide: "smartphones/digital media" names the exposure, "pathway" names how it would work, and "channel" or "margin" names what a research design identifies. Two structural consequences: H7 is retitled "Declining reading practice — H2's displacement pathway, measured directly," making explicit that it names a pathway rather than a rival root cause; and the section formerly titled "Mechanism Checks" is now "Channel Checks: Schooling Mode and Attendance," because what it tests are phase-two channels and a measurement alternative, not H2 pathways.

### 2. Asymmetric standards of evidence: accountability faces direct policy-variation tests while the smartphone hypothesis survives its own null

The review is right that the two null results received visibly different rhetoric in v1.9, and that is fixed structurally. It is mistaken about the underlying evidentiary situation on three specifics; rather than concede those, v2.0 makes the existing answers impossible to miss.

**Conceded and fixed: the rhetoric.** The waiver and 4G sections are now parallel to the point of being typographically matched. Matched titles: "NCLB waiver timing: no detectable effect on the release-timing margin" and "4G rollout timing: no detectable effect on the arrival-timing margin." The same five beats in the same order: what varies and why it identifies anything; the single margin the design identifies; the result with its minimum detectable effect against the relevant benchmark; which form of the hypothesis the null rejects; which channel survives by construction. Shared sentence frames in both sections ("The design identifies one margin:"; "The test is informative, not merely underpowered."). And mirrored closers:

> Whatever the accountability retreat did to American students' learning, it did not travel through the timing of a state's own release.

> Whatever digital media did to American adolescents' learning, it arrived with the device and its saturation of social life, not with the local cell tower upgrade.

The Sharper Tests introduction now states the symmetric standard as a contract before either test is run, and "What the tests jointly imply" applies it: both strong local forms are rejected by timing tests built from each hypothesis's own variation — the waiver design with a minimum detectable effect of about 3.2 points on bottom-decile grade 8 mathematics, less than half the gain the causal literature attributes to NCLB's adoption; the 4G design with minimum detectable effects of 0.013–0.016 SD, several times smaller than the cross-country 3G estimates — and in each case what survives, by construction, is a nationally synchronized channel that year effects absorb. Neither leading hypothesis leaves that section with a demonstrated local channel.

**Declined, with pointers to where the revision makes the answer explicit:**

1. *On the claim that the favored hypothesis got a pass on its null.* The 4G null was not an external finding the paper had to absorb; it is evidence this project generated against itself. The design was built, run, and reported by this project as an adversarial check on its own leading candidate — the first within-U.S. mobile-rollout design against achievement — and its null has been reported in the abstract since it was produced. v2.0 states that framing wherever the result appears: the abstract ("A county 4G-rollout event study, this project's adversarial check on its leading candidate, finds no detectable effect of local arrival timing"), the Sharper Tests introduction, the 4G section itself, and the H2 assessment.

2. *The claimed asymmetry in direct policy tests.* The smartphone hypothesis has a direct policy-variation test, and it was already in the paper: the quasi-experimental school phone-ban literature — staggered bans in England, Norway, and Spain, and Florida's 2023 statewide ban, with test-score gains concentrated among previously low-achieving students, the mirror image of the decline's signature — plus the pre-registered test of staggered U.S. state bans against NAEP 2026. What v1.9 failed to do was name this as the analog of the waiver test at the point where a reader would look for it. v2.0 does so twice: the Sharper Tests introduction ("The waiver event study is the direct policy-variation test of H3; the quasi-experimental ban literature and the pre-registered state-ban test against NAEP 2026 are the corresponding policy tests of H2"), and "What the tests jointly imply" ("That pairing is the smartphone-side analog of the waiver test: the same design logic pointed at the other leading hypothesis").

3. *The characterization of the waiver logic* ("we find no effect in waiver timing analyses, so accountability likely played a minor role at best" — while the 4G null is excused by a national-adoption channel). v1.9's waiver section already made the same national-channel concession, in nearly the same words the 4G section used: "If the operative change was a national shift in expectations and stakes culminating in ESSA, it hit all states simultaneously and is absorbed in year effects, invisible to this design." The two escape clauses were always parallel; the prose made one of them easy to miss. v2.0 makes them verbatim-parallel (the fifth beat in both sections, with matched clause structure), and the surviving weaker form of the accountability hypothesis is preserved explicitly: H3's assessment ends "leaving mainly its weaker national-climate form in play," and the Synthesis assigns the accountability retreat "a plausible supporting role," with its clearest remaining footprint at the public-school grade 4 bottom decile.

Because the two nulls are now symmetric, the paper also states plainly what the ranking rests on instead: "What still separates the two is positive evidence, not survival of elimination" — the adolescent-period incidence of the losses, the sector-neutral reading decline, the indifference to Common Core adoption, and the recurrence among adults across nineteen OECD countries ("What the tests jointly imply").

**On the international evidence** ("cross-national declines are also compatible with any number of other phenomena that are globally shared"): agreed, and the paper's design already reflected the point — international synchrony was never the argument by itself. It is one bar of a two-bar test: a candidate must clear the synchrony (including its extension to adults) *and* the local signature — bottom-of-distribution concentration, adolescent-period timing, sector neutrality — simultaneously (Synthesis, dual-criterion paragraph). Breadth eliminates candidates that lack it; it does not by itself elevate a candidate that has it. v2.0 sharpens this in exactly the direction the review presses: the Synthesis now names a second globally shared candidate that clears both bars — adolescent mental health, in its adolescent form — rather than presenting digital media as the unique survivor, and says outright that nothing in the report's evidence eliminates the rival. The accountability inference is also narrower than the review's paraphrase: internationally synchronized declines mean a U.S.-specific school policy cannot be the *sole* driver, not that it cannot contribute; H3's assessment retains "supported as a contributor."

### The writing point: "best explained by," "leave smartphones as the explanation most consistent with the full pattern," "verdict"

Conceded and executed globally. The phrases the review quotes were in the v1.9 abstract ("sharpen the verdict," "uniquely predicts," "the explanation most consistent with the full pattern of evidence"); none survives. "Verdict" is eliminated as a device and as a word (14 occurrences in v1.9, zero in v2.0); the per-hypothesis device is now "**Assessment**," written in calibrated language that names its licensing evidence. Headings no longer pre-announce winners: "H2: Smartphones and digital media — the best fit to the international, pre-pandemic facts" became "H2: Smartphones and digital media — timing, breadth, and the ban literature"; "H3: Accountability retreat — the best fit to the bottom-concentrated U.S. signature" became "H3: Accountability retreat — timing, the distributional prediction, and two direct tests"; "Public vs. Catholic schools: the decline is sector-neutral" became "Public vs. Catholic schools: an untreated-sector test." Where the paper still ranks, it uses the calibrated forms ("the explanation the tests consistently favor," "the best-supported remaining candidate"), with the pending-test qualifier attached.

## Changes beyond the review

Four sets of changes in v2.0 go beyond what the review raised.

**1. A corrected directional reading of the Minnesota Common Core contrast, found during this revision and disclosed in-text.** In re-verifying the Common Core section, a revision editor challenged v1.9's claim that Minnesota's +2.3-point grade 8 mathematics-minus-reading triple-difference was "the opposite of what the hypothesis that the Common Core harmed mathematics would predict for an untreated subject." Re-derivation against the archived results confirmed the challenge: a harm-to-mathematics account predicts a *positive* triple-difference, so the statistic in fact has the predicted sign — but it is driven by Minnesota's unusually large relative reading decline (−3.2 points), not by any mathematics outperformance, and the direct within-state contrast shows Minnesota's spared mathematics fell slightly *more* than other states' treated mathematics (−0.9 points at grade 8, −2.9 at grade 4), so avoiding the standards conferred no visible protection. No number changed; the inference did. The revised section reports the corrected reading and discloses the error: "an earlier version of this report misread that statistic's direction." The section's conclusion — the state-variation test fails to implicate the standards — stands on the never-adopter and repealer comparisons and on the corrected within-state contrast. Matching corrections were made to the public claims audit, the project state file, and the README (Sharper Tests, Common Core subsection).

**2. Primary-sourced evidence behind the mental-health rival.** The review named rising mental health problems in a list of alternatives; the revision treats the candidate with the same seriousness as the paper's leading hypothesis, on newly sourced national data: CDC Youth Risk Behavior Survey trends (persistent sadness or hopelessness among high-school students: 28% in 2011, 37% in 2019 — before the pandemic — 42% in 2021, 40% in 2023) and Mojtabai, Olfson, and Han's *Pediatrics* estimates (adolescent twelve-month major-depressive-episode prevalence: 8.7% in 2005 to 11.3% in 2014, stable through 2011 and rising thereafter). Both sources were fetched and verified during the revision, are cited in the report, and were added to the public claims audit with human-executable verification paths (rows B15–B16; the audit now covers 44 load-bearing claims).

**3. An age-profile reconciliation the review did not request.** v1.9 leaned on adolescent concentration while also citing the adult PIAAC decline as confirming evidence; the tension was never resolved. H2 now resolves it explicitly ("The age profile: a gradient, not a cliff"): exposure to the post-2012 digital environment is universal, but adolescents combine peak dose with the developmental window in which displaced reading, sleep, and attention compound into measured skill deficits, so the hypothesis predicts effects largest among adolescents and present but weaker among adults — which is what the data show — and the fertility literature's adult null concerns births, not skills.

**4. A full style and organization pass.** Beyond the calibration changes: the abstract was rebuilt at 300 words; em-dash constructions were cut from 161 to 21; sentences over roughly 40 words were split and paragraphs given topic sentences; the single-paragraph Limitations section was restructured into four thematic paragraphs (inferential status; data and measurement; the report's own designs; the composite digital-media evidence, ending with the unresolved mental-health entanglement); the Synthesis/Conclusion redundancy was removed (the Synthesis owns the ranking, the Conclusion owns implications); the set-asides subsection was moved to the end of Testing the Hypotheses so the candidate-triage arc runs continuously from the dispatches through the mental-health rival to the avenues set aside; figure placement and numbering were repaired; captions were made self-contained; and a roadmap paragraph was added to the Introduction. One rhetorical shortcut flagged by our own read rather than the review was replaced by the argument it stood for: "is, almost by construction, not an education-policy variable" is now "is not plausibly an education-policy variable: nothing education policy does reaches all of those populations at once" (Verifying the Decline, seniors-and-adults subsection). The compiled report grew by three pages (36 to 39).

## The designated adjudicator

The residual disagreement between this report and the review is about how much weight a consistency ranking can carry. More prose will not settle that, and v2.0 does not try. It states the ranking in calibrated terms and points at the test that can settle it: the staggered adoption of state school-phone bans — one state in 2023–24, five by 2024–25, roughly twenty-five to thirty by 2025–26 — evaluated against NAEP 2026, with the design and treatment coding specified in the project's public repository in advance of the data. This is the waiver design pointed at the other leading hypothesis: the kind of direct policy-variation test the review credits on the accountability side, now scheduled on the smartphone side. If binding bans move scores where and when they bind, particularly at the bottom of the distribution, the leading candidate gains its first direct U.S. policy corroboration. If they do not, the ranking loses its leading candidate, and the report is structured so that saying so cleanly requires revising one assessment, not the method. Either way, the adjudication is empirical, pre-specified, and outside the project's control.
